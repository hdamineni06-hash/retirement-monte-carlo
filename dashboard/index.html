Retirement Withdrawal Monte Carlo Simulator

Bootstrapped from 22 years of historical SPY/AGG returns — not a normal-distribution assumption — to stress-test how long a 60/40 portfolio survives a range of withdrawal rates, with traditional/Roth sequencing and SECURE 2.0 RMDs applied each year.

Methodology

  • Return simulation: annual returns for SPY and AGG are pulled via yfinance, blended 60/40 and rebalanced annually, then bootstrapped (sampled with replacement) to build 10,000 thirty-year market paths — no assumption of normally distributed returns.
  • Withdrawal sequencing: the portfolio is split into a traditional (tax-deferred) and Roth (tax-free) bucket. Discretionary spending draws from the traditional bucket first, then Roth, so the tax-deferred balance — and future RMDs — shrink faster.
  • RMDs: starting at age 73 (SECURE 2.0), the traditional bucket is forced to distribute at least the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table amount each year, regardless of spending need. Any distribution beyond that year's need is reinvested after tax.
  • Taxes: traditional withdrawals and RMDs are taxed at a flat 22% federal rate; state tax is ignored. Roth withdrawals are tax-free.
  • Spending: the withdrawal rate sets year-1 spending as a percent of the starting portfolio; spending then grows with 2.5% assumed inflation each year in real terms.

Built with Python (NumPy bootstrap Monte Carlo, pandas, yfinance) and Chart.js. Source data cached from Yahoo Finance.
